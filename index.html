The Challenge

Growing ridership outpaces capacity — technology can bridge the gap safely.

Overcrowding Risk

When demand exceeds seats — and sometimes safe capacity — crowding at doors and aisles raises the risk of pushing, stumbling, and falls, endangering all passengers.

Timing Mismatches

Crowded stops lengthen boarding and alighting time. Delays accumulate along routes. Commuters lack crowd-level and ETA information to decide whether to wait or board.

Seat Insufficiency

Demand grows faster than available seats. Fare-paying and free-travel passengers share the same limited seating. SmartSeat AI improves visibility and safety for all commuters, without prioritizing by gender or fare status.

Human Impact

Pressure on drivers and conductors, boarding conflicts at busy stops, falls on crowded or moving buses. SmartSeat AI emphasizes prevention and safer boarding for everyone.

Policy Background

Understanding the demand context behind SmartSeat AI

Stree Shakti — Free Bus Travel for Women (Andhra Pradesh)

The Stree Shakti scheme, launched on 15 August 2025, is one of the Super Six promises by the Andhra Pradesh government. It provides free bus travel for women on APSRTC and private-stage carrier buses across the state.

Objectives

  • Enhance women's mobility and freedom of movement
  • Support financial independence by reducing transport costs
  • Improve access to education, employment, healthcare, and essential services
  • Empower women across urban and rural Andhra Pradesh

The scheme has significantly increased public bus usage, creating a genuine need for better capacity management, real-time passenger information, and enhanced onboard safety.

Reported figure: Approximately 12 lakh free rides were recorded in the first 30 hours of the scheme's launch.

Verification note: The launch date of 15 August 2025 and the "12 lakh rides in 30 hours" figure are reported statistics. Readers should verify these against an official government press release or credible news source before citing.

SmartSeat AI is positioned as a complementary technology that helps transport operators manage growing demand — it does not evaluate or comment on policy decisions.

Implementation Roadmap

A phased, realistic path from prototype to scale

Phase 1 — Pilot (Months 1–3)

Small-Scale Validation

Install 360° cameras and edge devices on 5–10 buses on a single high-demand route (e.g., RTC Complex–Gajuwaka in Visakhapatnam). Validate person-counting accuracy, test driver alert UX, and collect baseline data.

Phase 2 — Refinement (Months 4–6)

Model Training & Integration

Train prediction models on collected data. Integrate with APSRTC GPS/ETM feeds. Launch passenger-facing app (crowd info + ETA). Address accuracy and connectivity issues from pilot.

Phase 3 — Scale-Up (Months 7–12)

Multi-Route Expansion

Expand to 50–100 buses across 10+ routes. Deploy central analytics dashboard for depot managers. Implement automated fleet rebalancing suggestions.

Phase 4 — Full Deployment (Year 2+)

State-wide Rollout

Integrate with state transport management systems. Scale to hundreds of buses. Open anonymized data APIs for researchers and urban planners.

Risks & Mitigations

🔒 Privacy

Risk: Camera footage may raise privacy concerns.
Mitigation: Edge-only processing. No faces stored. Only anonymized counts transmitted. Compliance with IT Act and data protection norms.

🎯 Accuracy

Risk: Counting errors in crowded or low-light conditions.
Mitigation: Multi-modal validation (camera + door sensors). Continuous model retraining. Confidence scores on all predictions.

💰 Cost

Risk: Hardware and connectivity costs per bus.
Mitigation: Use affordable edge devices (Jetson Nano ~₹15K). Phased rollout. Seek government/CSR funding for pilot.

📶 Connectivity

Risk: Inconsistent mobile network in rural areas.
Mitigation: Offline-capable edge processing. Batch sync at depots. Local driver alerts work without connectivity.
